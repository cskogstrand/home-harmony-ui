# Notifications

The bell distinguishes startup, unavailable sources, disabled notifications and a confirmed empty result. Active alerts remain accessible when another source is unavailable. A known state sensor reporting `unknown`, or an optional trigger attribute without a value, can be waiting for its first reading. The bell indicates the wait and the Notifications page names the affected rule. Today reserves source warnings for actual failures, including missing or unavailable trigger entities and failed calendar or event subscriptions. Affected rule names, retry and notification settings are available with the warning.


Open **Home settings → Notifications** to add rules using **Add notification** in the relevant group, including an empty group. Under **Display as**, choose **Banner** for a short summary, **Section** for grouped details, **Activity cards** for prominent readings and progress, or **Compact activity card** for the same information in less space. **Show on** selects **Today only** or **All main pages**: Today, Calendar, Lists, School and Smart home. The default is a section on all main pages. Both choices are shared across screens and included in backups.

On the selected main pages, notifications appear in a grid just below the page title. Tiles share the height set by **Widget grid zoom**. A printer can show its live frame beside the time left and progress bar. An ongoing task shows its name and any configured progress or remaining time. Current alerts show the event title and start time, an all-day label, or the rule’s title and message. A configured activity camera, person snapshot or image is visible without tapping. Tap a tile to open its details in a dialog. The tiles keep their size and place. **Close**, Escape or tapping the backdrop returns to the board. **Dismiss notification** clears a current alert for this activation.

Camera popup rules, including doorbells, still open their viewer automatically on every page. After closing it, tap the camera thumbnail or its title to reopen it while the alert is active. The separate dismiss button clears that activation. Alongside ongoing media and image previews, the board shows up to three compact rows, with camera alerts first, then current alerts and ongoing tasks. **View all** and the header bell open the complete list.

To test the board, open **Settings → Debug → Show all notifications** (**Innstillinger → Feilsøking → Vis alle varsler**) and choose **Go to Today**. The board shows every saved rule, including disabled rules, with its configured layout, media and current readings. Active alerts keep their event details. The preview lifts the three-row limit, and you can tap each tile to inspect its popup. The bell continues to count real alerts. Tap **Previewing all notifications** to return to the switch. Turning it off or reloading the tab ends the preview.

| Type | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Ongoing tasks | Shows when an entity state or attribute matches. Clears when all show conditions stop matching, or when its configured hide condition and delay take effect. |
| Current alerts | Shows a matching state, calendar event, or momentary event. State alerts use the same hide options as ongoing tasks. Calendar alerts clear at the event's end. Dismiss hides that activation on this screen. |
| Camera popup | Opens a selected camera when a condition becomes true or a new event arrives. Closes after the configured duration or when dismissed. |

Rules, titles, messages and selected entities are saved in shared settings and included in settings backups. Each rule and the whole notification system can be switched off. No rule is enabled until you add it.

The notification area fades in or out over 160 ms. When it changes above the viewport, the page keeps the reader’s place. If a cleared alert contained keyboard focus, focus returns to the header bell. Dismissed controls become inactive immediately.

## Choose triggers

Under **When to show it** (**Når varselet skal vises**), use **Add trigger** (**Legg til utløser**) to configure up to 10 triggers for one notification. **Any** trigger can activate it. For example, one camera popup can respond to both a doorbell event and a motion sensor. Each trigger has its own source, condition or event filters, and can be removed while at least one remains. Existing single-trigger rules keep working, and extra triggers are shared across screens and included in backups.

By default, state alerts stay visible while at least one state condition matches. Camera state triggers react independently: a second sensor becoming active can open the camera even while the first is still active. All camera and momentary-event triggers in a rule share its cooldown and duration; simultaneous matches create one timed alert. Calendar events remain separate alerts, and mixed current-alert rules keep their state, calendar and timed alerts independent.

For an ongoing task or current state alert, open **When to hide it** (**Når varselet skal skjules**) and enable **Use a separate hide condition**. Choose the source, optional attribute, comparison and value. An active card then stays visible through intermediate states. The first match of the hide condition starts **Hide after (minutes)**. Choose 0 for immediate removal or a whole number up to 1,440. Repeated readings and unrelated state changes preserve the deadline. A fresh show trigger before expiry starts a new activation if the hide condition has cleared. If both conditions match, hiding takes priority. The hide condition only affects the state alert in a mixed rule.

For a printer, choose its status sensor once under **When to show it**, choose **Equals**, and enter `prepare`. Use **Add value** (**Legg til verdi**) to add `running` and `pause`. Any listed value can show the card. Existing **Equals** triggers for the same entity and attribute open together in the editor. Each value can be edited or removed individually and counts toward the limit of 10 triggers per rule. Use **Add trigger** to set a different source or condition. Set the hide condition to **Equals** `finish` on that sensor and choose 5 minutes. The card stays visible if the printer goes idle during those five minutes. Starting another print gives it a fresh activation.

Entity fields use the shared [entity picker](entity-picker.md). Choose a device to narrow the list, choose **All entities** or **Without a device**, or search directly by name, entity ID, room or integration. Calendar fields show supported calendar entities. Camera selection is separate and lists your configured cameras. Unavailable saved entity selections stay visible until you replace or remove them.

- **Entity state:** choose an entity, an optional attribute, and Equals, Does not equal, Above or Below. Use the raw Home Assistant value shown in the editor, such as `on`, `running`, or a numeric power threshold. Missing/unknown readings do not satisfy ordinary conditions; an explicit Equals `unavailable` or `unknown` condition can report those states.
- **Calendar event:** choose a calendar and how many minutes before its start to show the alert, from 0 to 1,440. Each overlapping event gets its own alert, including all-day events in the home's timezone. The calendar does not need to be assigned to a person in the planner.
- **Event entity:** choose a Home Assistant `event` entity. Each new timestamp triggers once; optionally match its reported `event_type`. This is commonly used for doorbells. [Home Assistant event documentation](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/event/).
- **Home Assistant event:** enter the event-bus type and optional exact top-level JSON filters. For example, `{"action":"single"}` only matches that action. Use filters to select the intended device if several devices emit the same event type. Empty filters match all events of that type. Nested filters and templates are not supported. [Home Assistant WebSocket events](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/api/websocket/#subscribe-to-events).

Momentary events and camera popups have a duration and cooldown. Repeated triggers during cooldown are ignored; later triggers replace the same rule's active alert and restart its duration. Different camera alerts queue until closed or expired. Tap the camera picture to reopen its viewer until the alert expires. Dismissing the alert hides it entirely.

The camera popup header counts down to its expiry in minutes and seconds. While a state trigger still matches, it shows “Closes automatically when this alert expires.” instead. The configured expiry still applies; clearing the trigger does not restart the countdown.

Under **Entities and readings**, use **Add entity** to choose an additional reading, such as an appliance's remaining minutes or completion percentage. Its display name and placement are configured in the same section. Each selected reading appears in the banner and notification list with its reported value and unit. Use the remove button beside a reading to remove that additional selection. Entities also used by the trigger or progress stay available for those roles. Existing rules keep their previously selected reading; these choices are shared across screens and included in backups. Readings keep their reported values; optional progress is configured separately below.

## Entities and readings

In **Entities and readings**, set a local display name for every selected source, additional reading or progress entity. State and attribute readings can have different names and placements, even when they belong to the same entity. Leaving the name empty uses the entity name. Home Assistant names and references remain unchanged. Configured camera names are managed under **Home settings → Cameras**.

Choose **Under the title**, **Left reading**, **Right reading**, **At the bottom**, or **Hidden**. Activity cards show left and right as large readings in separate columns, with each visible name above its value; this also applies to compact activity cards. Other styles show them as text. **Automatic** keeps the source under the title, places the first two additional readings left and right, and places the rest at the bottom. Entities selected only for progress start hidden. Hidden readings still drive triggers and progress. Names and placements are saved with the rule and included in backups.

## Progress for ongoing tasks

Enable **Show progress bar** in an ongoing task. Choose a **Current value** entity and optional numeric attribute, then configure **Start value** and **End / total value** using either fixed numbers or entities (with optional attributes). Enabling progress preselects the numeric trigger source or the first numeric additional reading and uses a numeric trigger threshold as the end value; otherwise the end defaults to 100. Review these values before saving.

- Completion percentage: current percentage entity, start 0, end 100.
- Completed units: current count entity, start 0, end the entity reporting the total.
- Remaining time: remaining-time entity, start the total-duration entity, end 0.

All three values must use the same unit. Progress follows `(current − start) / (end − start)` and is limited to 0–100%. Both directions and decimals are supported. Missing, unavailable, nonnumeric or equal endpoint readings show an unavailable message rather than invented progress. The trigger still controls when the task appears and clears; reaching 100% does not change Home Assistant or override the trigger.

Progress appears in banners, sections and activity cards. It updates with entity readings, and its configuration is shared across screens and included in backups. Existing rules keep progress off.

## Activity cards and previews

The detail dialog follows the rule’s **Display as** choice. Banners and sections present readings in a list. Activity cards use the saved subtitle, left, right and footer positions. Compact activity cards use tighter spacing. Hidden readings and hidden labels follow the same rules as the full card. On phones, paired media columns stack when space is tight. Each picture opens its own larger viewer.

Calendar dialogs show the occurrence title, date and start time or all-day label in the home’s time zone. Timed event alerts show when the trigger fired. An ongoing task can show time left, progress, either one alone, or just its message and readings. The progress track spans the detail area, with the percentage and remaining time above it when enabled. Each sensor pairs its name with its value and unit. On narrow activity cards the readings wrap within their chosen positions. A doorbell popup puts its configured video or person snapshot first, with any extra readings and image below it. Closing the camera returns to the previous view. An alert that ends also closes its details.

Choose **Display as → Activity cards** for a row of task cards. Cards share a heading line and align their progress bars along the bottom. A card with an image or camera gets more width when space allows. Narrow screens stack the cards. Under **Icon and animation**, search or filter by category to choose from every device family supported by the dashboard, including robot vacuums and robot lawnmowers. Appliance variants include dryers, ovens, refrigerators, heat pumps and EV chargers; cover variants follow [Home Assistant cover classes](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/cover/), with sensor choices for temperature, humidity, motion, smoke, gas, water leaks and more. Existing icon selections are preserved. Automatic keeps the generic icon for the notification kind. Ongoing tasks can animate their icon; the per-rule switch and the device's reduced-motion preference can keep it still. The chosen icon also animates in the editor’s icon picker so you can see its motion before saving. Current and camera alerts use static icons. Slow, icon-only motion indicates activity: robots travel, fans spin, covers lift or swing, and water and signal icons flow or fade. Loops pause offscreen and in background tabs. Readings and text remain stationary. Timestamp readings show the reported time in the home's timezone; no completion time is estimated.

Open **Preview notification** on the settings page to see all saved rules together, grouped by type, including inactive and disabled rules. Expand **Display as** below any preview to choose its style; this saves that rule's display choice automatically and updates the preview. With no saved rules, the modal shows a built-in example. The gallery starts with sample data.

In **Edit notification**, **Board widget** and **Popup window** both preview the current draft as you edit. The widget shows the tile as it appears below the page title. The popup shows the details, including the camera viewer for doorbell rules. Tap the tile or **Open full size** to inspect the popup at its normal size. Changes take effect on the board when you save.

Under **Home settings → Notifications → Widget grid zoom**, choose **Small**, **Medium** or **Large**. Small uses compact rows and text. Medium (the default) enlarges widget text by 10% and Large by 20% relative to Small. The same zoom choice sets the column width and row height. On a phone where only one column fits, all three choices still have distinct heights. The board and editor preview each fit as many columns as their available space allows. This setting is shared across screens and included in backups.

Under **Default widget width**, choose **Automatic**, **1 column**, **Up to 2 columns** or **Up to 3 columns**. The sketches show the widths at the same height. Automatic uses one or two columns according to the content. On narrow screens each widget uses only the columns that fit.

Under **Board widget → Edit widget → Widget width**, use the shared default or choose a width for that rule. Changes to the default apply to all widgets that follow it. Existing per-widget choices carry over. Select **Use default width** to return a widget to the shared setting. Both settings sync across screens and are included in backups.

Set a separate widget title and short text, or leave them empty to use the defaults. **Widget media** can follow the notification, show no media, or use its own image, live camera or person snapshot. Under **Widget content**, choose **Custom lines** to set the widget’s text area from top to bottom. Each line has its own **Add field** button. Place up to three fields side by side. When a line has several fields, **↑** beside a field moves it one place to the left on the widget and **↓** moves it one place to the right. Each can show free text, a sensor value or status, a progress bar or time remaining. The remove button beside a field removes just that field. The arrows in the line header move the entire line with all its fields. The editor always offers four positions. The fourth is marked **Large zoom only** and appears on the board and in the preview only at Large zoom. Small and Medium show the first three positions. Use **Field name (optional)** to give any field a label of your own. Clearing it restores the default label. **Hide name** shows just the value or bar while keeping the label available to screen readers. These choices apply in both layouts. Lines without fields are skipped. **Side-by-side fields** shows the first field from each of the first three lines in one row beneath the title. The other fields are available again when you switch back to **Custom lines**. **Automatic** uses the notification defaults. For example, a printer widget can show print progress, nozzle temperature and time remaining together. The tile keeps its chosen size as the readings update.

A progress field accepts a sensor reporting 0–100 or the notification’s configured progress range. Time remaining accepts a duration sensor or finish timestamp, and can follow an ongoing task’s time source. Existing widget readings occupy the first positions in their saved order. **Widget and popup** sets the common defaults. **Popup layout** controls the detailed presentation and reading positions. Widget overrides are stored with the rule and included in backups.

The preview starts with current readings, media and the matching active calendar occurrence when available. **Use sample data** substitutes example readings, calendar events and media placeholders. For rules with progress, drag **Demo progress** or press **Play demo**. Reduced motion keeps the slider available and disables playback.

The editor fills 85% of the visible screen height. On a large screen, setup sits beside both previews. On a phone, switch between **Setup** and **Preview** at the top. Existing rules open with collapsed sections summarizing the trigger, title, media, readings and presentation. For a new rule, **When to show it** and the empty title field start open. A new camera rule also opens **Camera and image**. The **Use in notification** shortcut preselects the camera. Several sections can stay open, and collapsing one keeps its values. **Save notification** checks the whole draft, opens sections that need attention, and offers a link to each invalid field. Save and Cancel stay in reach in either view.

## Typical setup

1. For a washing machine, add **Ongoing tasks**, choose its operating-state entity, and match the state it reports while running. A power sensor with **Above** can also work; use a Home Assistant helper if the appliance's power fluctuates during a cycle.
2. For current plans, add **Current alerts**, choose **Calendar event**, select a calendar, and set the advance notice.
3. For a doorbell, add **Camera popup**, select its event entity or a binary sensor that turns `on`, then select the camera. Person-detection sensors work the same way.

Current alerts have an **Image** section for an optional Home Assistant image. Camera rules and ongoing tasks have a **Camera and image** section. **Camera** lists only cameras explicitly added in **Home settings → Cameras**. Each camera has a name, a connection (Home Assistant or a saved go2rtc server), and a source. Configure reusable servers in **Home settings → Camera connections**. HA camera discovery alone does not add cameras to this picker.

For a 3D printer, add an **Ongoing tasks** rule that matches its printing state, select **3D printer** under **Icon and animation**, and choose the printer camera under **Camera and image → Camera (optional)**. Select live video under **Show in notification**. **Show live preview below the page title** defaults to on for ongoing tasks with live cameras in all four presentation styles. Switching it off shows the camera when the detail dialog opens. The active preview shows the camera immediately on phones and wall screens, alongside the configured progress bar and remaining time. Tap anywhere on it to open the configured activity dialog with its progress, time left, readings and optional model image. Tap the camera in the open card for its viewer. **Close** or Escape returns to the preview. The preview and its open details clear together when the rule hides the task.

Under **Progress → Time left sensor**, choose a remaining-duration sensor or a finish timestamp. **Automatic** looks for a remaining-time or finish-time reading among the rule’s additional entities, then checks for a progress range measured in time units. A percentage alone cannot supply a finish time. Missing data shows as unknown, and a dashed track indicates unavailable progress. The popup duration does not limit ongoing tasks. Choose **No camera** to remove the feed. Missing saved cameras must be replaced or removed before saving. Camera choices and the printer icon sync across screens and are included in backups.

**Show in notification** chooses that camera's live video or its configured **Person snapshot**. **Test camera output** previews the choice. **Configure camera (new tab)** opens setup without losing the notification draft. **Smart home → Cameras → Use in notification** preselects the camera in a new rule. See [camera setup](cameras.md). A snapshot may predate the alert and refreshes while visible to pick up delayed images.

Under **Camera and image → Image (optional)**, select a Home Assistant `image.*` entity for a separate image, such as the model being printed. The selection supports both Home Assistant registry IDs and unregistered entity IDs. It updates when the entity changes and uses the existing authenticated image proxy; no image files or URLs are stored in the rule. **Image placement** defaults to **Left reading** and also offers **Right reading** and **At the bottom**. Images in reading columns fit inside a 16:9 frame, including tall model images. Transparent outer space is removed automatically before fitting the image, with a small margin around every visible pixel so the whole model remains visible. This also applies to **Test image output** and is recalculated when the image source updates. Opaque images keep their original framing. Images above 4 MP, blocked pixel reads and failed crop loads fall back to the original image. A light backing behind the image keeps dark models visible through transparent pixels, including in dark mode and the explicit image test. Opaque pixels retain their original appearance. Click or tap the image to open it in the shared full-size media viewer, with the same transparent-border trimming and light backing. The image stays up to date while open; close with the close button or Escape to return focus to the image. Ending the task removes its viewer. The read-only editor preview shows the same image at card size. Choose **No image** in the picker to remove it without changing the camera. Missing entities stay selected until replaced or removed; failed images offer **Retry**. **Test image output** also shows the selected image while editing its settings. Image choices and placements sync across screens and are included in backups.

Under **Camera and image → Camera placement**, choose **Right reading** to show a compact camera in the right measurement column of activity cards, including compact activity cards. Choose **Left reading** to put it on the other side. For a printer, use **Image placement → Left reading** and **Camera placement → Right reading** to show the model and live progress side by side. Each column keeps its readings below the media. Tap the image or video to enlarge it. The editor preview shows the selected feed in that position. **At the bottom** preserves the full-width camera below the readings and remains the default for existing rules. Banners and sections show both media below the readings. The choice applies to video and person snapshots, syncs across screens and is included in backups.

Both activity card styles use equal 16:9 frames for side-by-side image and camera previews. In a **Compact activity card**, the text sits beside the previews on wide cards. On narrow cards the previews sit below the title and subtitle. When enabled, the compact card shows the progress percentage beside the bar.

In the compact camera view, the playback status sits at the top corner with a 12px glyph, an accessible name and a 44px touch target, without adjacent text. Its explanation and retry actions remain available in the status popover. If a person inset occupies the top-left corner, the status control moves to the top-right.

Camera notification cards show the selected video or person image inline in every presentation, including banners and compact activity cards. Live video uses the camera's shared, prefetched still image while connecting, including its configured Frigate latest-image source. This cache refreshes every five seconds while the tab is visible and survives closing the viewer. Tapping the camera picture opens the larger viewer using the same live playback session as the card. Both visible surfaces show the same frames; closing and reopening the popup does not reload video or repeat the WebRTC/MSE connection. Offscreen cards release their view, and the stream stops when no visible view needs it or the tab goes into the background. Person images reuse the shared page cache, prefetched when saved settings load and refreshed by HA entity updates even with no open viewer; direct Frigate sources poll while the tab is visible. The editor uses that same visible-camera lifecycle. Selecting **Use sample data** replaces the feed with a placeholder.

Under **Camera and image**, enable **Show last person on card** to overlay the configured latest person image on that rule's live notification card. It defaults to off and is saved with the rule, shared across screens and included in configuration backups. Fullscreen live previews always show the inset when a person source is configured. Person-only notifications keep their full image without a duplicate inset. Tap the inset for image status or retry; it may predate the alert.

Change **Person inset size** under the camera's **Person image (optional)** settings. Illustrated choices show Small (16%), Medium (24%, the default), Large (36%), and Extra large (48%) as a share of the displayed video's width, excluding empty space around it. The same proportions apply to all notification rules and fullscreen previews using that camera. The inset follows the person image's own format without added bars; tall images scale down proportionally when needed to fit inside the video.

## Connection and privacy

Notifications run while the dashboard is open and connected. They do not send phone push notifications, email, or messages, and cannot catch events while the browser is closed or suspended. Active state/calendar notifications are reconstructed on load; old doorbell events and camera triggers are not replayed on initial load, reconnection or rule edits. After a reload or reconnection, a matching show condition can activate the card again. Pending hide countdowns belong to the previous session. Sensor recovery from `unavailable` does not open a camera.

Alert content, dismissals and camera views remain in this browser session. Reloading clears dismissals. Home Assistant events, event history and camera images are not written to settings storage. go2rtc cameras use authenticated signaling with an HTTP MSE fallback; Home Assistant cameras retain their video, MJPEG and labeled snapshot fallbacks. Background tabs pause live playback. Connection loss clears active alerts and stops camera playback.

Choose devices from your own Home Assistant instance in the editor. Keep real household identifiers and event filters out of repository examples and screenshots.

The Notifications page keeps household alerts first. Device availability is a collapsed diagnostic summary with separate unknown and unavailable counts, grouped by physical device or integration. Expanding a group reveals its entity links. Offline status is explicitly marked as cached.
