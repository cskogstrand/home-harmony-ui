# Design System: Afterglow

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Liquid Glass household board"**

Liquid Glass inspired controls float over a readable household board. Cool pearl ground, white work surfaces and blue actions keep the light theme crisp; midnight ground and pale blue actions carry the same hierarchy in dark mode. A softly illuminated rim and restrained shadow distinguish navigation from the content underneath.

The interface retains aligned rows and opaque reading surfaces. Independent settings groups use separate cards with icon headings; related content rows can use dividers within their shared surface. Apple system typography leads the existing font stack, with self-hosted Public Sans as the fallback. Density changes with the available space; touch actions, readable labels and explicit states remain visible.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Pearl and midnight grounds with blue actions.
- Glass navigation, header controls and transient menus over opaque content.
- System typography, rounded capsules and a clear reading hierarchy.
- Responsive density, generous portrait touch controls and solid accessibility fallbacks.

This records the built system in [src/index.css](src/index.css), [src/afterglow.css](src/afterglow.css), the shared shell and UI components. The source uses Tailwind v4 theme mappings in CSS. YAML values describe the default light theme; tokens prefixed with `dark-` record matching dark overrides. The sidecar extends these tokens with states, motion and framework-free previews.

The experiment follows the separation of controls and content described in [Apple's Materials guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials) and [Meet Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/). The web implementation approximates that material with CSS translucency, blur, highlights and shadows. It does not reproduce native lensing, optical refraction or automatic background-driven material adaptation.

The iOS 27 refinement follows [Apple's readability and contrast updates](https://www.apple.com/os/ios/): quieter rims, stable selection fills and denser text menus. The web app approximates material adjustments with its own dialog glass controls. It retains the bottom dock for shared wall-display reach instead of adopting iPadOS's native top tab bar.

## Colors

The palette uses cool neutrals with a blue action color. Ambient blue and warm tints soften the app ground without tinting the reading panels.

### User-selectable palettes

Settings → Screen and alerts → Appearance offers **Pearl** (the default blue), **Sage** (muted green), **Sand** (warm earth), **Dusk** (soft violet), **Mist** (soft blue-gray), **Rose** (dusty pink), **Olive** (earthy yellow-green), **Clay** (muted terracotta), **Lavender** (blue-violet), **Seafoam** (soft teal), **Honey** (warm ochre), and **Slate** (cool gray). Each has an independent light and dark treatment, selected with the existing dark-mode switch. The palette remaps global surface, ink, action, focus, navigation, ambient and shadow tokens through `data-color-palette` on the document root. Semantic status colors and person assignments retain their meaning.

The labeled radio choices show the current mode's actual background, card and primary tokens. Selection and keyboard focus have visible outlines. Choices form three rows of four on wider screens and wrap to two columns on phones. They use the existing shared settings, conflict recovery and backup flow; older settings default to Pearl.

The separate Artwork picker previews the SVG illustrations in the active palette and mode. No artwork keeps the original ambient background and is the default for new installations and older backups. Selected artwork fills the wall at 26% opacity on every page, with a panoramic crop behind the header. The decorative layer ignores touch and is hidden in forced colors, increased contrast and reduced transparency. Controls and headings that sit directly on the artwork get one opaque card-colored surface per control or row, never a patch behind a single label: tab and segment containers, the Smart home navigation and section heading row, and unselected filter pills. Selection over artwork uses the primary fill and semibold labels, never a card-on-card fill.

### Card artwork

Appearance offers a palette-colored background scene and heading illustration for each planner card type. Morning summary, Waste collection, Next family plan, Everyone’s day, Everyone’s week and Home favorites decorate the corresponding Today section. Calendar decorates the navigation and the active calendar view. The four list types share their choices across Today and Lists, including homework on School. Timetables decorates the Today school strip and each timetable on School. The scene fades in at the lower-right corner, leaving the card’s original surface behind most text. Next family plan uses the same solid panel as the other cards, so its title, time and description can sit directly on the surface. Person avatars and assigned shopping icons keep their identity.

Each configured Smart home card has its own switch, including multiple cards of the same type or entity. The card has a woven corner illustration and a palette-colored badge containing its device glyph. The compact scene button displays the badge beside its action label. The editor preview reflects the saved choice. Generated device tiles on detail pages and individual Today favorites use their existing device icons.

Camera feeds and person snapshots, Sonos covers, vehicle imagery, notification state icons, calendar event markers and Home Assistant status readings convey content or state. Their existing renderers retain ownership of those visuals. Configuration panels keep their heading icons. Illustrated cards default off and restore their ordinary icons in forced colors and increased contrast.

Device card icons, controls, sliders, selected segments, control popovers and decorative illustrations use the selected palette's primary, accent and surface tokens. Card rims and shadows use the shared glass tokens. Card types have no separate fixed color categories. Light-color controls and color-picker samples retain their actual hues; light previews mix any supplied lamp color into the current card surface, with palette-colored controls and text. Without a supplied light color, the preview uses the palette's primary color. Semantic status colors and configured person colors remain independent of the palette.

Shared range thumbs and filled tracks fall back to the palette's primary color outside device cards; thumb borders fall back to the opaque card surface. Loading device-card styles must not make Sonos sliders transparent.

### Primary

- **Action blue** (`primary`): primary buttons, links, selected filters, current-day emphasis and focus.
- **Navigation blue** (`nav-active`): the active route's text and translucent selection fill; `nav-muted` and `nav-foreground` distinguish resting and hovered links.
- **Dark-theme sky blue** (`dark-primary`): the lighter action color used over midnight surfaces, with a dark foreground on filled buttons.

### Neutral

- **Pearl ground** (`background`) sits behind **white work surfaces** (`card`) and the cool `popover` layer.
- **Blue-black ink** (`foreground`) carries task content; `muted-foreground` is for supporting text.
- `secondary`, `muted` and `accent` are cool tints for secondary controls and selected or hovered surfaces. `border` divides groups; `input` gives editable controls a stronger edge.
- `media-backing` is a fixed light gray behind trimmed transparent images (printer models, device pictures). It has no dark counterpart and ignores the palette, so dark models stay visible in both themes.
- `nav-background` and `nav-border` define the glass control layer's fill and rim. `glass-highlight` and `glass-shade` provide its light and shade; `ambient-blue` and `ambient-warm` belong to the app ground.
- The `dark-` counterparts preserve the same roles over midnight ground. Dark navigation has its own fill, ink, rim and highlight values.

### Status and assignment

`destructive` and `warning` provide red and amber feedback; `success` and `info` provide green and blue feedback. Each has its own foreground token. People use their configured color as a runtime value, repeated in avatars and softly tinted event borders; person colors are not brand primitives. Existing safety, online and pending roles reuse the corresponding semantic status colors in the CSS.

**The Meaning Before Color Rule.** Pair selected, offline, loading, error and completion colors with text, icons or semantic state; color is never the only cue.

## Typography

**Display and body font:** the shared `--font-ui` stack selects `-apple-system`, then `BlinkMacSystemFont`, self-hosted Public Sans and generic sans-serif. This gives Apple platforms their system typography while preserving the bundled fallback elsewhere. Public Sans normal weights (400–700), Latin and Latin Extended WOFF2 files, `font-display: swap` and the OFL license remain in `public/fonts`; no external font request is required.

The frontmatter records the whole type ramp. Its anchor roles are the light-weight display clock, semibold page headlines, panel titles, regular supporting copy and medium compact labels; the other steps fill the gaps between them. The Today heading uses a slightly tighter line height (1.2). Times and primary numerical readings use tabular numerals. Page descriptions stop at a readable measure (65ch).

Sizes set in `em` (units beside a large numeral, text inside a scaled widget) and fluid `clamp()` headings derive from a step and stay relative. Tailwind's own `text-*` utilities in shared UI primitives keep their built-in scale.

**The Ramp Floor Rule.** No visible text goes below 11px: content stops at Caption (12px), and Navigation caption (11px) is reserved for labels that sit inside a larger named target. Notification widget zoom multiplies Body large by 1.1 and 1.2 rather than adding steps.

**The One Reading Format Rule.** Home Assistant readings go through the shared reading helper (`reading`/`withUnit` in `src/lib/i18n.ts`), so one view never mixes separators or precision. Numbers use the dashboard language's separators and minus sign (`−2,4 °C` in Bokmål, `-2.4 °C` in English). Precision follows the entity's Home Assistant display precision (the user's setting, then the integration's suggestion); without one, temperatures show at most one decimal, W and Wh none, and kW/kWh two decimals below 10 and one from 10. Other values keep their reported digits. A no-break space separates every unit from its number in both languages; `%` and a bare angle `°` attach to it. Large dial and readout numerals that set the unit in a smaller element keep that composition.

For the large portrait display, the root uses 125% of the browser’s default text size (normally 20px), the clock uses (4.5rem), the Today heading (2.25rem), panel headings (1.375rem), list labels and dock links (1.125rem), event titles (1rem), and supporting event times (.875rem). This yields readable content around (18–28px). At phone width, the clock uses (3rem), the Today heading (1.5rem) and page title (1.75rem). Phone dock captions use the 11px navigation caption step; their full links remain large touch areas.

**The Read Before Decoration Rule.** Use the existing sentence-case heading hierarchy and concise action labels. Give content the strongest typographic emphasis.

## Layout

The shared header, main content and footer are centered within a maximum width (1600px). Default page gutters and panel padding use the frontmatter spacing tokens. The main board has two equal flexible columns; major sections span both. Reusable forms, rows and toolbars wrap instead of relying on fixed content widths.

Home Assistant connection issues appear in a banner below the header with a link to connection settings. A healthy connection takes no space in the header.

| Context | Built behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Large portrait: width ≥1000px, height ≥1400px, portrait | Root type grows to 125%, with 2.8rem page gutters, 1.2rem panel padding and a 1rem board gap. Favorite and scene rows center their final items at the same width as a full row. |
| Wide landscape: width ≥1100px, landscape | Board uses six tracks; the week spans four and lists two. |
| Width ≤999px | Gutters reduce to 1.5rem and school cards stack. Scene and favorite rows use the space available inside their panel. Dock icons sit above 12px captions, aligned even when labels wrap. |
| Width ≤800px | Device rows put controls below the title and retain readable metadata. |
| Width ≤699px | Gutters and panels use 1rem; the board and primary grids stack; navigation floats within .75rem side margins, with icons above labels. |

Everyone’s day shows one 4rem avatar centered above each person’s name. Today and Tomorrow share those columns, with one date heading per row of people. When the card narrows, the next group starts below both days. Month view uses the shared event cards with a two-line title, time and person. Tapping an event opens its full details. Phones show the plan count on each date and open its agenda.

The weekly grid uses horizontal overflow when necessary, with a sticky person column. A 63rem minimum keeps narrow layouts scrollable. From 700px the person column narrows to a 4rem avatar column: the name and the Family column heading become visually hidden text, the row header keeps the name as its accessible name, and the avatar carries a name tooltip. Phones keep the visible name beside their sideways scroll. At 1440 × 2560, all seven dates fit beside that column, with room for a start and end time on one line at the default reading scale. Multi-day events remain aligned across dates. Week-grid titles break only between words or at hyphenation points in the table’s language, which follows the active app language; they never fall back to breaking anywhere. They clamp at two lines with an ellipsis; the button’s accessible name and the event dialog keep the full title. Each person row has one outlined add button beneath the avatar, labeled with the person’s name; it opens the editor for that person on today, or on the week’s first day when today is out of view, and the editor chooses the day. Adding on a specific day goes through the day header, which opens that day with its own Add a plan. Today’s compact week has no add controls. Content below the viewport stays reachable by scrolling.

Calendar timetables use five equal weekday columns. At a container width of (620px) or less, days stack with dates beside their events in flexible one-to-two columns. Full-width messages remain beneath the week.

Sonos shows one destination per reported playback group, with room names and current track/state. A single centered player follows, with artwork beside the track and controls on wide screens. Short desktop windows cap the artwork column at (20rem); tall portrait screens retain the larger cover and shared reading scale. Phones use a (96px) cover beside the track, with full-width transport and volume below. **Your music**, **Group settings** and **Speaker settings** open shared dialogs. The music dialog uses the viewport height minus (2rem), with a fixed toolbar and scrolling collection. Favorites use two columns of readable rows with square (64px) thumbnails, reducing to one column on phones. Destination cards scroll horizontally when needed; **Change room** closes the library and focuses that selector.

The dock is centered within the page gutters, capped at (1000px), or (1328px) on large portrait screens. Its bottom margin is (1rem) plus the safe-area inset; phones use (.75rem) plus the inset. The shell measures the combined navigation and optional Sonos mini player height. App bottom padding and keyboard scroll clearance reserve that height plus (2rem) and the bottom safe-area inset. Links are at least (56px) high by default, (72px) on large portrait displays and (64px) on phones. Recurring action controls use a (48px) minimum where explicitly styled; portrait event and list rows reach (64px). Compact mode reduces panel gaps and padding while retaining usable control targets.

The shell respects top and side safe-area insets. The dock uses independent left/right safe gutters, and keyboard scroll padding includes the bottom inset. Phone form fields use 1rem text to avoid focus zoom at the default text size.

Synthetic-data captures in `.impeccable/review/liquid-glass/` cover desktop, phone, portrait, dark mode, menus and dialogs. They support visual review; physical touchscreen and target-device performance still need device validation.

## Elevation & Depth

Opaque work panels use a thin cool border and tonal separation. Glass adds depth to the floating dock, camera shortcut, notification bell, settings control, dropdown menus, popovers and dialogs. Its base is a solid navigation fill with restrained inset edges and a soft outer shadow. Header controls use a smaller outer shadow than the dock. Broad specular gradients are omitted to keep labels clear.

Within `@supports`, backdrop filtering adds blur (24px) and saturation (1.45), with fill opacity (.62) in light mode and (.68) in dark mode. Menus and popovers use denser fills (.72 light, .76 dark), with secondary text using the stronger navigation ink. Dialogs and confirmation dialogs use a lighter fill (.30), blur (20px), saturation (1.5) and a black backdrop at (.20) in both themes. Settings → Screen and alerts → Appearance → Advanced appearance → Modal glass provides transparency (0–85%), frost (8–48px), saturation (100–200%) and backdrop dimming (0–60%) sliders with a live inline preview. Values save on release through the shared settings/conflict/backup flow; keyboard adjustments save immediately. Reset glass appearance restores only these four defaults. Older configurations receive the defaults. The timetable day dialog follows the same material; its inline timetable frames retain their own styling. Browsers without backdrop-filter support retain solid surfaces. Reduced transparency and increased contrast use opaque fills, remove blur and all glass shadows, strengthen the border, and flatten the ambient ground. Increased contrast also outlines selected navigation and segments. Forced colors use system Canvas and ButtonText without blur; the current dock route and selected segments gain a Highlight outline.

The sidecar records the three soft elevation tokens plus glass and header shadows. Navigation selection uses an opaque secondary fill without another glass layer or shadow. Dialog shells and action rows share one continuous glass surface. Form fields retain their opaque reading surfaces; long editor bodies scroll independently above the actions, without a separate footer fill or divider.

**The Glass Control Layer Rule.** Reserve glass for the main dock, header controls and transient menus, popovers and dialogs, with one local exception: calendar timetable frames reuse the shared panel glass on School, dashboard previews and the expanded view. Keep the regular calendar, lists, form fields and other recurring work panels opaque; saved image/PDF timetables retain their existing presentation.

### Reusable device cards

Smart home, Today's favorites and `/device-gallery` share the device card library in `src/components/devices/`. `CardFrame` provides the glass surface, type icon, title, room, and three densities. `CardLayouts` contains light, climate, cover, power, security, media, fan, humidity, water heater, robot, valve and 3D printer layouts; `CardControls` supplies accessible actions, readings, ranges, choices and secondary-control disclosure. The media layout owns transport buttons and the placement of seeking, volume and extra controls so demo and live cards stay consistent. Transport controls share the full card width with space between actions. `card-designs.ts` maps Home Assistant domains and helper aliases to the library families. ## Shapes

The system pairs rounded work panels with capsule controls. Work panels use the `panel` radius, planner fields the `field` radius, transient menus the `menu` radius and standard dialogs the `dialog` radius. Shared component radii use a fixed 16px base, so portrait typography does not change their corners. ### List motion

List changes use deliberately slower, flowing motion so people can follow what changed. Rows, surrounding items and the panel surface move together, without a stagger or a pause between exit and reflow. Keep this pace distinct from the short control and overlay transitions above.

| Change | Motion | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switch list or tab | Reveal the selected rows within the moving card edge. A second selection redirects the transition from its current height. Keyboard navigation and reduced motion change lists immediately. | 600ms, `cubic-bezier(.77, 0, .175, 1)` |
| Added or reopened | Fade in from 24px to the right and 97% scale; highlight the row. | 800ms, `cubic-bezier(.77, 0, .175, 1)` |
| Edited | Move 12px to the right, then settle back as the highlight fades. | 800ms each direction, same curve |
| Completed | Show the confirmed check and strikethrough; slide 24px right, shrink to 97% and fade out. | 700ms, `cubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1)` |
| Deleted | Slide 24px right, shrink to 97% and fade out, preserving the previous checked state. Remaining rows reflow alongside the exit, including inside Completed. | 700ms, `cubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1)` |
| Reflow | Move remaining rows, the completed section and empty state; resize the panel surface alongside them. | 800ms, `cubic-bezier(.77, 0, .175, 1)` |
| Highlight | Primary color at 14% opacity, fading in and then out. | 350ms in; feedback clears after 800ms, followed by a 750ms fade |
| Check | Fade in and scale from 90% to full size. | 450ms opacity, 600ms scale, shared `--ease-out` |

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** reuse semantic CSS colors so the dark theme follows the same roles.
- **Do** keep the portrait reading scale and large list, event and navigation targets.
- **Do** preserve visible focus, text wrapping, scrollable overflow and safe-area clearance.
- **Do** provide solid surfaces for reduced transparency, increased contrast and unavailable backdrop filtering.
- **Do** show unavailable, pending and empty states distinctly.
- **Do** keep examples fictional and household data outside committed design assets.

### Don't:

- **Don't** shrink or hide task content to force a tall screen into one viewport.
- **Don't** encode a person, device state or selected route through color alone.
- **Don't** spread glass beyond the documented control layers and calendar timetable and Smart home card exceptions, or add decorative gradients or elevation to other recurring work panels.
- **Don't** turn a timetable preview into a cropped substitute for the full, pageable view.
- **Don't** describe CSS blur and specular edges as native optical refraction.
